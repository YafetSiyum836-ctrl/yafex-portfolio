A marketing website built to turn visitors into users.

01 — Problem Definition

How Do You Sell
An Experience?

The app gives users a hands-on experience — they can feel how it works. The website has no such advantage. It has to earn trust and communicate value through structure, visuals, and words alone, in the first few seconds.

Background

DailyLoop is a habit-tracking mobile app designed to help users build better routines, stay consistent, and track progress over time. While the app delivers the core experience, users still need a clear and engaging way to discover the product before deciding to download it.

The Challenge

Unlike the app, where users interact directly with features, the website must translate that experience into content, visuals, and structured navigation — communicating value quickly without the benefit of hands-on use.

The Goal

Design a marketing website that clearly presents DailyLoop's core features, communicates its value, and encourages action — showcasing the product, building trust, and creating a smooth path from discovery to download.

01
Communicate value fast — visitors decide within seconds whether a product is worth exploring further.
02
Make the product feel real — screenshots and previews must do the work that hands-on interaction does inside the app.
03
Build trust before download — testimonials, polish, and clarity stand in for the confidence usually earned through usage.
04
Guide without overwhelming — repeated CTAs should feel like natural next steps, not pressure tactics.

Key Insights From Research

Seconds, Not Minutes

Visitors make decisions quickly, so the product's value has to be obvious within the first few seconds of landing on the page.

Show, Don't Just Tell

Visual previews of the product communicate features faster and more convincingly than text alone — screens do the explaining.

Trust Closes The Gap

Testimonials, product highlights, and polish increase confidence before downloading — replacing trust that usage would normally build.

Marketing Strategy

Unlike the mobile app, the website's main goal isn't interaction — it's conversion. The experience needs to capture attention, build trust, and drive action, so the structure and content were planned around that shift from the start.

Research Focus

  • Feature presentation — how products explain their value clearly
  • Call-to-action placement — how visitors are guided toward sign-up or download
  • Trust-building — how testimonials, visuals, and social proof are used

Design Approach

  • A strong hero section communicates value immediately
  • Feature sections explain how the app works, with clear visuals
  • Product previews make the experience feel real and tangible
  • Testimonials build credibility before the ask
  • Download CTAs placed strategically across the page
03 — Define

Conversion
Goals

To support product discovery and improve conversion, the website was designed around six clear goals — each shaping a different part of the page structure and content.

Increase App Downloads

Create clear, repeated call-to-action points that encourage visitors to download the app.

Build User Trust

Use product previews, clear messaging, and testimonials to increase confidence in the product.

Explain Product Value

Present features and benefits simply, so visitors quickly understand how DailyLoop helps them.

Improve User Engagement

Create an engaging browsing experience that keeps visitors exploring the product and learning more.

Support Decision Making

Provide enough information, visuals, and context to help visitors make a confident download decision.

Strengthen Brand Identity

Carry DailyLoop's visual language from the app into the website for a consistent, recognizable brand.

CTA
Placement
Challenge
Visitors may be interested but lose momentum if the download action isn't immediately visible or easy to reach.
Strategy
CTAs were placed across high-attention areas — the hero, feature highlights, and final section — instead of relying on one download block.
Outcome
Multiple conversion points let users act whenever they're ready, reducing friction and increasing download opportunities.
Trust
Signals
Challenge
Before downloading, visitors need confidence that the product is reliable, useful, and worth their time.
Strategy
The site integrates social proof — testimonials, product previews, and clear messaging that show the interface and real feedback.
Outcome
Trust signals reduce uncertainty, strengthen credibility, and improve the likelihood of conversion.
Feature
Presentation
Challenge
Poorly explained or overly complex features can cause visitors to miss the product's value entirely.
Strategy
Features were broken into focused sections supported by visuals and short explanations — easy to scan, easy to understand.
Outcome
A clearer presentation helps visitors connect functionality with their needs, making the download decision easier.
